HIGH IMPACTMobile + Desktop

The header and sticky bars crowd the screen

1 / 8
Before · first mobile screen

What's wrong

  1. 1Four header icons from different families, including an eye and a phone with no labels
  2. 2A full-width search bar with "AI Mode" on a page the shopper has already found
  3. 3A scrolling promo ticker about custom orders
  4. 4A sticky tab bar for four sections far below the fold
  5. 5A floating call button over the price
  6. 6A sticky Custom Quote / Net Terms / Chat bar across the bottom of every screen

About 45% of the first screen is store navigation and pinned bars. The rest is taken by text.

Why it matters

Pinned bars shrink the space for the product on every scroll, and unlabeled icons make shoppers guess. The page feels like a B2B portal, not a store.

How I'd fix it

One slim header (menu, logo, search, wishlist, cart) in one icon family. The promo shrinks to one short line. The tab bar, call bubble and B2B bar go. The only thing pinned is the Add to cart bar.

Desktop6 utility links, 6 header items, an 8-item menu and 3 promo banners sit above the title. The fix keeps one nav row with the B2B links grouped at its right end.

The product image isn't the first thing a shopper sees

2 / 8
Before · what loads above the photo

What's wrong

  1. 1The page opens on text: a 3-line title split by the SKU and "Click To Copy", then Item #, Brand and UPC
  2. 2Then stars, "26 people purchased", price, "Save up to" and stock
  3. 3Only then the photo, about 750pt down, with the sticky B2B bar cutting across it
  4. 4The "gallery" is four thumbnails of the same picture

Shoppers have to read a block of codes before they can see what they're buying.

Why it matters

The photo is the first thing shoppers look for. It confirms "is this the charger I want?" before they read a word. Here they can't confirm the product, the cable or the plug without scrolling, and the duplicate thumbnails make the page look unfinished.

How I'd fix it

Image card right under the header, filling the first screen: the full product, then close-ups of the vehicle plug and the charge panel, with swipe, dots and full-screen view. A "Batteries not included" pill sits on the photo. Compare, Save and Share sit just below, then the title and price.

03
HIGH IMPACTMobile + Desktop

"Will it fit my batteries?" has no clear answer

3 / 8
Before · rows from the spec table
① 12/24V in, 18V out… or "12/18"?
② 30–60 minutes or 3 hours?
③ For M18… but series M12?
④ Batteries in the photo: are they included?

What's wrong

For a charger, the one question that matters is "does it fit my battery and my truck?". The answer is spread over a 33-row table that contradicts itself five times:

  1. 1Input 12/24V and output 18V, then "Voltage 12/18"
  2. 2Charging time "30 to 60 min", then "Time to charge: 3 hours"
  3. 3"For use with M18" and "Series M12", while the title says M12/M18 and the first feature names one 18V pack
  4. 4The only photo shows two batteries. Nothing says whether they're in the box

Cord length is also listed as 6.3 ft and 6 ft, and dimensions appear twice with different numbers.

Why it matters

A shopper who isn't sure it fits won't buy, or buys and returns it. Each contradiction makes every other claim on the page harder to believe.

How I'd fix it

"Works with" chips under the title (M12, M18, 12V/24V DC) and a plain "Charger only, batteries sold separately" note above the buttons. Below them, one clean set of highlights and specs, with conflicts resolved with the supplier.

Add to cart competes with 14 other actions

4 / 8
Before · the buy box on mobile

What's wrong

  1. 1The only buy button, about 1,140pt down the page
  2. 2Affirm and PayPal squeezed next to it, one cut off mid-word
  3. 3"+ Add to Wishlist" as an underlined text link
  4. 4"Buy in Bulk & Save - See How" and "Watch Now"
  5. 5Get Volume Pricing in a red outline, louder than Add to cart
  6. 6Get Custom Quote
  7. 7B2B Net Terms, Safe Payment, Ask Question
  8. 8A cancer warning right under the buy box

Add the sticky bar and call bubble: 14 other tappable things. Once you scroll past the button, there's no way back to it.

Why it matters

Every extra option slows the decision (Hick's law). Everyday buyers wonder whether they need an account or a quote to buy one charger.

How I'd fix it

One clear pair of actions: a blue Add to cart and an orange Buy Now. A sticky bar keeps Add to cart reachable on every scroll. Pay-later sits in one tidy card, and business options move below the buttons as three quiet cards.

MEDIUM IMPACTMobile + Desktop

Price and delivery raise questions instead of answering them

5 / 8
Before · price and shipping

What's wrong

  1. 1"SAVE UP TO 22.76": no $, no original price, no condition. It reads like a discount on this unit
  2. 2…but it's actually a bulk saving, hidden behind "Buying In Bulk"
  3. 3"QUICK SHIPPING*": the asterisk leads nowhere

Nowhere on the page is there a shipping cost, a delivery date or a ZIP check, and the price sits in green next to a green "save" badge.

Why it matters

Unexpected costs are the top reason shoppers abandon a purchase in Baymard Institute's checkout research. For a $227 charger, "what will it cost me, and when do I get it" decides whether it goes in the cart.

How I'd fix it

A plain dark price with the quantity stepper beside it. One honest line: "Save up to $22.76/unit on bulk orders · See pricing". Real demand and stock, Item #, Brand and UPC with copy icons, and "Delivered by Tuesday, Nov 3" with a ZIP the shopper can change.

MEDIUM IMPACTMobile + Desktop

The reviews are about the store, not this charger

6 / 8
Before · top of page, then the reviews

What's wrong

  1. 1Five stars and "See reviews" right under the title
  2. 2"4.8 Overall Rating" with no review count
  3. 3Each review shows a single star and talks about delivery
  4. 4Others cover back orders, refunds, order tracking, a shower repair and an RV cabinet hinge

These are Shopper Approved reviews of the store. Not one is about this charger. "Related Videos" is a generic DK Hardware brand video, too.

Why it matters

Reviews are how shoppers check fit and quality. Stars that turn out to describe something else feel like a trick, and a shopper who spots it starts doubting the rest of the page.

How I'd fix it

Split proof by what it's about. For the product, "No reviews for this charger yet · Write". For the store, a labelled "DK Hardware store rating 4.8 · rates the store's service, not this charger". Real signals move up: 9 answered questions, 30-day returns, 5-year warranty.

After · honest proof
